Sources

Where the briefing comes from

Every figure in the residents' briefing is drawn from a document that is already public. This page lists them, so anyone can check the briefing against the originals.

Most of the documents below are collected in one place — the resident-run site whyhopefield.co.za, where the scoping reports, specialist studies, public notices and correspondence can be downloaded. This page is a guide to what those documents are; the briefing quotes them, and where they disagree with each other the briefing says so.

  • DFFE National Web-based Environmental Screening Tool reports — for the fuel-plant site (generated 2 June 2026, under “Dangerous Goods,” “Emissions,” “Indigenous Vegetation” and “Wastewater Treatment” activity classifications) and for the solar site (15 April 2026).bound into the scoping reports as appendices · on whyhopefield.co.za

Specialist studies

Commissioned by the developer and lodged with the applications. All are on whyhopefield.co.za.

  • Biodiversity Impact Assessment — report A3BA23-018, issue date 6 February 2024 (field survey November 2023). Underpins the biodiversity sections of both the fuel-plant and solar applications; appears in two versions with the same reference and date, one describing a 900 MW facility and one a 1.6 GW facility.
  • Social Impact Assessment — scoping-phase report, and a separate Social Impact Assessment Baseline Report dated January 2025.
  • Visual Impact Assessment — November 2023.
  • Glare Assessment — ref GLA-172-01, 20 September 2024 (ForgeSolar modelling).
  • Traffic Engineering Scoping Report — version 1, 1 December 2023.
  • Aquatic Impact Assessment — wet-season survey, July 2026 (surface water only).
  • Soil & Agricultural Potential — an “Agricultural Compliance Statement” (solar appendix F).
  • Heritage Impact Assessment — Phase 1, fuel plant, field survey January 2025 (also carried as the solar application's heritage study, appendix G).
  • Request for Exemption of a Palaeontological Impact Assessment — solar site, 14 June 2024.
  • Geohydrology (“Desktop Geohydrological Assessment”), traffic, visual and climate-change inputs to the fuel-plant Final Scoping Report — summarised in that report; the standalone versions are not all public.

Responses from government bodies

All three organ-of-state responses below are bound into the solar Final Scoping Report at appendix D4.

  • Heritage Western Cape — Response to Notification of Intent to Develop. Requires a full section-38(3) Heritage Impact Assessment, including an archaeological assessment, consideration of alternatives, and community consultation.ref HWC23103026SB0507
  • CapeNature — comment on the solar Draft Scoping Report, 9 July 2026. States that it “objects to this application” and that the site plan is “fatally flawed from a biodiversity perspective.”ref SSD14/2/6/1/8/4/_167
  • Western Cape Department of Agriculture — comment, 10 July 2026, copied to DEA&DP and Saldanha Bay Municipality. States it is “unable to provide informed comment” while the layout is undefined, and that “no site alternatives have been identified or assessed.”ref 20/9/2/2/11/128
  • Air Force Base Langebaanweg (SA Air Force, Department of Defence) — letter of 28 April 2026 to the Saldanha Bay Municipal Manager, headed “objection to the application.” States that “glare risk is governed by geometry … not distance” and asks that “approval be withheld pending the completion and review of the required aviation and environmental assessments.”ref AFB LBWG/R/402/16
