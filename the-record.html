Kortliks

The bottom line

Kort opsomming

Phelan Green, a renewable-energy company, proposes a roughly R47-billion complex north and east of Hopefield: a synthetic-jet-fuel plant and a solar farm of more than 3,000 hectares, on land holding threatened fynbos and renosterveld, in a water-scarce catchment whose groundwater flows toward Langebaan Lagoon. The core government decisions have not been taken. The public consultation so far has been widely criticised as rushed and split into pieces. There is a real window to change the project or the conditions attached to it — and it is open now.

Phelan Green, 'n hernubare-energiemaatskappy, stel 'n ongeveer R47-miljard kompleks noord en oos van Hopefield voor: 'n sintetiese-straalbrandstofaanleg en 'n sonkragplaas van meer as 3 000 hektaar, op grond met bedreigde fynbos en renosterveld, in 'n waterskaars opvanggebied waarvan die grondwater na die Langebaan-lagune vloei. Die kern regeringsbesluite is nog nie geneem nie. Die openbare deelname tot dusver is wyd gekritiseer as haastig en in dele opgebreek. Daar is waarlik 'n venster om die projek, of die voorwaardes daaraan geheg, te verander — en dit is nou oop.

The three concerns with the strongest documentary basis:

Die drie kwelpunte met die sterkste dokumentêre grondslag:

  • Water. The fuel-plant scoping report rates groundwater contamination as “high significance” and confirms the aquifer under the plant “would drain towards the Langebaan Lagoon” — yet, where the government screening tool requires a full field-based Hydrology Assessment, the report proposes only a desktop study, and the contractor who prepared the groundwater input did not visit the site.
  • Alternatives. Both scoping reports decline to assess alternative locations — the solar report's stated reason is that “the applicant currently owns the farms.” South Africa's environmental law requires alternatives, including site alternatives, to be assessed, and Heritage Western Cape has separately required it.
  • Fragmentation. One physical complex is being assessed as six or more separate applications, so no single study weighs the combined effect on water, wildlife, traffic or air. The solar report's own table lists about 18 approved renewable energy projects within 30 km of the proposed development, and limits “cumulative impact” to changes in the landscape that affect people's view of it.
  • Water. Die brandstofaanleg se bestekverslag gradeer grondwaterbesoedeling as “hoë belang” en bevestig die akwifeer onder die aanleg “sou na die Langebaan-lagune afloop” — tog, waar die regering se siftingstelsel 'n volle veld-gebaseerde Hidrologiese Evaluering vereis, stel die verslag net 'n lessenaarstudie voor, en die kontrakteur wat die grondwater-inset voorberei het, het nie die terrein besoek nie.
  • Alternatiewe. Albei bestekverslae weier om alternatiewe liggings te bepaal — die sonkragplaas-verslag se gegewe rede is dat “die aansoeker tans die plase besit”. Suid-Afrika se omgewingswet vereis dat alternatiewe, insluitend terrein-alternatiewe, geassesseer word, en Erfenis Wes-Kaap het dit apart vereis.
  • Verbrokkeling. Een fisiese kompleks word as ses of meer afsonderlike aansoeke geassesseer, dus geen enkele studie weeg die gekombineerde effek op water, wildlewe, verkeer of lug op nie. Die sonkragplaas-verslag se eie tabel lys ongeveer 18 goedgekeurde hernubare projekte binne 30 km, en beperk “kumulatiewe impak” tot hoe dit die uitsig sal verander.

What is on the record

Wat op die rekord is

“Phelan Green” is the Phelan family's renewable-energy group. Its subsidiary Solar Capital is the applicant for the Hopefield solar farm; Phelan eFuels Saldanha is the applicant for the fuel plant. Near Hopefield they propose a single integrated complex, applied for as several separate projects. The figures below are the developer's own, from the scoping reports and public notices; where they disagree with each other or with residents' comments, that is noted.

“Phelan Green” is die Phelan-familie se hernubare-energiegroep. Sy filiaal Solar Capital is die aansoeker vir die Hopefield-sonkragplaas; Phelan eFuels Saldanha is die aansoeker vir die brandstofaanleg. Naby Hopefield stel hulle 'n enkele geïntegreerde kompleks voor, waarvoor daar as verskeie afsonderlike projekte aansoek gedoen is. Die syfers hieronder is dié van die ontwikkelaar self, afkomstig uit die bestekverslae en openbare kennisgewings; waar dit met mekaar of met inwoners se kommentaar bots, word daarop gewys.

Core idea
Solar electricity splits water into green hydrogen (it is “green” because renewable solar energy, not grid or fossil-fuel power, is used to make it); the hydrogen is combined with captured carbon dioxide to make synthetic jet fuel (e-SAF) for export. The carbon dioxide is to come from biomass boilers “owned and operated by a third party” — a combustion plant that is not part of this application. The developer's 2025 letter said the carbon would be extracted “from straw, procured by local farmers,” and a later trade-press interview described invasive alien plants instead. In a written reply to a resident (22 September 2026), the fuel-plant consultant clarified on the developer's behalf: the biomass plant will be owned and operated by Ithemba BioEnergy, a separate project with “its own independent environmental authorisation process”; the feedstock is woody biomass “with invasive alien trees as the primary feedstock,” plus agricultural residues including wheat straw; and about 100,000 tonnes a year is needed for Phase 1, with another 100,000 for Phase 2. Where that biomass would come from has not been stated, and no application for the biomass plant has been found in the public record. Claimed
Location
Farmland north and east of Hopefield, west of the Sout River. The developer repeatedly told residents ~3 km from town; residents' comments say the nearest boundary is ~900 m; the solar Final Scoping Report now states the project is “approximately 1 km north of Hopefield town (Garden Street)” and that it “borders along the existing Hopefield Wind Farm in the south-east.” Contested
Solar farm
The study area is given as anywhere from 2,880 to 3,338 hectares across different documents; the number of farm portions as three or five. Capacity appears as ~1.6 GW, 900 MW, “13 phases,” “four phases,” and — on the applicant's own proposed-layout drawing — three phases (340 / 605 / 655 MW). Plus a large battery and three on-site substations. Mainly to power the plant, with surplus sent to the national grid: the Final Scoping Report has power carried by a 132 kV line to the Aurora substation, with surplus exported to the grid. (Earlier documents said it would not be connected to the grid.) Contested
Land condition
One account describes about 30% of the solar site as existing grassland or old fields and the rest as Hopefield Sand Fynbos classed as a Critical Biodiversity Area, within the Swartland Protected Agricultural Area — not uniformly pristine, but mostly high-value. Contested
Fuel plant
About 61 hectares. Built in three phases — 35,000 tonnes/year (2028), +35,000 (2030), +70,000 (2032) — totalling 140,000 t/yr of synthetic jet fuel. About a 25-year operating life; structures up to ~40 metres high; an LPG-fired flare. Documented
Hazardous inventory
On the plant site, from the scoping report's own table: about 300 t of hydrogen, 1,500 t of liquid carbon dioxide, 450 t of oxygen, 6,400 t of naphtha, 420 t of LPG, ~35,000 t of fuel storage, 1,000 t of potassium hydroxide, ammonia and amine. Potassium hydroxide (caustic potash) is singled out because it is the largest stock that is not a fuel or gas, and its risk is different: it will not burn or explode, but it is strongly corrosive. It is most likely the liquid electrolyte for the electrolysers that split water into hydrogen; it causes severe burns to skin and eyes and is harmful to aquatic life. Here, the concern is a leak or spill soaking into the “highly permeable” aquifer under the plant, which the report itself says drains towards Langebaan Lagoon (see the water concern). A hazard-and-operability study and a Major Hazard Installation risk assessment are required — deferred to the impact-assessment phase. Documented
Water
The documents give five incompatible accounts of the water source. The fuel-plant report: about 8 million litres a day gross (“3–5 million net”) from the Hopefield municipal drinking-water line, plus groundwater. A January 2025 social report: seawater desalination (under its own separate assessment) and, in the same document, treated municipal wastewater. The developer's May 2025 letter: “retreated effluent water from the Saldanha Wastewater Treatment Plant” (a different town). A paid advertisement in the Weslander (27 August 2026), marked as such: the plant is “zero-liquid-discharge”, and a desalination plant being investigated would “pump water back into the municipal water system”. Brines and salts are trucked off-site. Contested For scale: Saldanha Bay Municipality’s own Water Services Development Plan puts the whole of Hopefield’s water use at about 0.74 million litres a day (2016, for about 7,300 people). On the fuel-plant report’s own figures, the plant would use about eleven times as much water as the whole town (gross), or four to seven times as much after recycling, and about a quarter of the entire municipality’s 2016 water use. Documented
Transport
Fuel moved by road tanker ~40–50 km to the Port of Saldanha. Construction: about 44,500 truck trips over five years and ~400 workers a day at peak. A new ~5 km sewage pipeline to the Hopefield treatment works. Documented
Investment
Headline figure R47 billion. The company has publicly described the project at ~$800 million (about R13 billion), ~$2.5 billion (about R41 billion) and ~$2.6 billion (about R43 billion) at different times. None of these dollar figures matches the R47 billion headline: converted to rand, the company's own figures range from about R13 billion to about R43 billion. Calculated The product has shifted too: the 2023 status letter and the developer's own consultant reports as late as January 2025 describe a green-ammonia plant; the developer's May 2025 letter calls it a shipping fuel plant; only the June 2026 scoping report reframes it as synthetic jet fuel. Contested New, 15 September 2026: the Presidency announced the project has reached Final Investment Decision, with $100 million (about R1.7 billion) in equity committed and an offtake agreement, construction targeted for the first quarter of 2027 and first exports for early 2029 — on the government's own account, the most advanced of six national “first-wave” green hydrogen projects. Documented That announcement sits oddly against a separate government publication released the same month, the national Green Hydrogen Deal Book, which rates this same project's Final Investment Decision as still “less than 6 months” away — two government sources disagreeing, within weeks, on whether the decision has actually been made. The fuel itself — and whether there is a market

Die brandstof self — en of daar 'n mark is

The finished synthetic jet fuel is ordinary kerosene — certified and handled exactly like the Jet A-1 airlines already use. It has a high flash point and is one of the safer liquid fuels to store; tanks of finished fuel are not the main hazard. The genuinely dangerous materials sit upstream, inside the plant: hydrogen, carbon monoxide, process intermediates, LPG, naphtha and the potassium hydroxide. The scoping report acknowledges risks of “fires and explosions, as well as asphyxiant releases” and defers the Major Hazard Installation study and the hazard-and-operability review to a later stage.

Die voltooide sintetiese straalbrandstof is gewone paraffien — gesertifiseer en hanteer presies soos die Jet A-1 wat lugrederye reeds gebruik. Dit het 'n hoë vlampunt en is een van die veiliger vloeibare brandstowwe om te stoor; tenks vol voltooide brandstof is nie die hoofgevaar nie. Die werklik gevaarlike materiale sit stroomop, binne die aanleg: waterstof, koolstofmonoksied, prosestussenprodukte, LPG, nafta en kaliumhidroksied. Die bestekverslag erken risiko's van “brande en ontploffings, sowel as verstikkende vrystellings” en stel die Groot-Gevaar-Installasie-studie en die gevaar-en-bedryfbaarheidsoorsig na 'n latere fase uit.

There is a market. The EU's ReFuelEU Aviation law sets a synthetic-fuel quota airlines must meet every year, rising over time, and the UK has its own mandate. Supply is well short of it — as of mid-2026, of more than 40 announced European synthetic-fuel projects, none had reached a final investment decision. A plant that is built and delivering would have a guaranteed premium market. Set against that, the wider green-hydrogen sector has contracted sharply: less than 4% of globally announced hydrogen capacity due by 2030 had entered construction, several major energy companies (including BP, Air Products, Shell and Fortescue) have exited hydrogen megaprojects on economics alone, and no gigawatt-scale African green-hydrogen project had reached a final investment decision as of August 2026.

Daar is 'n mark. Die EU se ReFuelEU-lugvaartwet stel 'n sintetiese-brandstof-kwota wat lugrederye elke jaar moet behaal, wat mettertyd styg, en die VK het sy eie mandaat. Voorraad is ver te kort — teen middel-2026 het geeneen van meer as 40 aangekondigde Europese sintetiese-brandstof-projekte 'n finale beleggingsbesluit bereik nie. 'n Aanleg wat gebou is en lewer, sou 'n gewaarborgde premie-mark hê. Daarteenoor het die breër groenwaterstofsektor skerp gekrimp: minder as 4% van die wêreldwyd aangekondigde waterstofkapasiteit wat teen 2030 in werking moes wees, het die konstruksiefase bereik; verskeie groot energiemaatskappye (waaronder BP, Air Products, Shell en Fortescue) het hulle om suiwer ekonomiese redes aan grootskaalse waterstofprojekte onttrek; en teen Augustus 2026 het geen Afrika-projek vir groenwaterstof op gigawatt-skaal nog 'n finale beleggingsbesluit bereik nie.

The real risk to Hopefield is delivery risk. The finished product is ordinary jet fuel, so if an export deal falls through it can be sold to domestic airlines instead — it is not a hazardous stockpile that must sit on the site. The risk is that the land is cleared and the authorisation won, then the project stalls at Phase 1 or slips for years. A market for the fuel is not the same as a funded plant: synthetic jet fuel costs several times more to make than fossil kerosene, so lenders want signed long-term purchase contracts at a price that covers that cost before they will finance construction — and the $100 million (about R1.7 billion) in equity announced so far is a small fraction of a R47-billion build. If that financing does not close in full, the result is the ecological damage without the investment and jobs at the promised scale. The described product has changed three times in three years. Regulation

Regulering

One project, many applications

Een projek, baie aansoeke

One physical complex has been divided into roughly six separate authorisation processes, each with a different decision-maker, a different consultant and a different comment deadline. That is lawful — but it has consequences.

Een fisiese kompleks is in ongeveer ses aparte magtigingsprosesse opgedeel, elk met 'n ander besluitnemer, 'n ander konsultant en 'n ander kommentaar-sperdatum. Dit is wetlik — maar dit het gevolge.

“Final Scoping Report” — what it is, and what it is not

“Finale Bestekverslag” — wat dit is, en wat dit nie is nie

The environmental-authorisation route being used here has two reporting stages. Stage 1 is the Scoping Report: a Draft goes out for comment, then a Final Scoping Report is submitted to the competent authority, which has about 43 days to accept or reject it. Only then does Stage 2 begin: the specialist studies, a Draft Environmental Impact Report for at least 30 days' public comment, a Final report, and the decision. So “Final” means the finalised scoping-stage report — not the last document and not the last chance to comment.

Die omgewingsmagtigingsroete wat hier gebruik word, het twee verslagfases. Fase 1 is die Bestekverslag: 'n Konsep gaan uit vir kommentaar, dan word 'n Finale Bestekverslag by die bevoegde owerheid ingedien, wat ongeveer 43 dae het om dit te aanvaar of te verwerp. Eers dan begin Fase 2: die spesialisstudies, 'n Konsep-Omgewingsimpakverslag vir minstens 30 dae se openbare kommentaar, 'n Finale verslag, en die besluit. So beteken “Finale” die gefinaliseerde bestekfase-verslag — nie die laaste dokument nie en nie die laaste kans om kommentaar te lewer nie.

And there is a Final Scoping Report per sub-project, not one for the whole complex — the solar farm; the fuel plant; the hydrogen / methanol campus and its 132 kV line (described by the applicant as “a further, separate EIA,” not started); the desalination plant (“its own Environmental Impact Assessment,” not started); the wind facility (not started); and the Water Use Licence and Atmospheric Emission Licence, which are licensing processes with their own public participation. Several of the not-yet-started applications will each run a full scoping-then-impact-report cycle, each with its own comment periods.

En daar is 'n Finale Bestekverslag per sub-projek, nie een vir die hele kompleks nie — die sonkragplaas; die brandstofaanleg; die waterstof-/metanol-kompleks en sy 132 kV lyn (deur die aansoeker beskryf as “'n verdere, aparte OIB”, nie begin nie); die ontsoutingsaanleg (“sy eie Omgewingsimpakstudie,” nie begin nie); die windkragfasiliteit (nie begin nie); en die Watergebruiklisensie en Lisensie vir Atmosferiese Vrystellings, wat lisensiëringsprosesse met hul eie openbare deelname is. Verskeie van die aansoeke waarmee daar nog nie begin is nie, sal elk 'n siklus van omvangbepaling gevolg deur 'n impakverslag deurloop, elk met sy eie tydperke vir kommentaar.

The municipal land-use process — separate from the environmental one

Die munisipale grondgebruikproses — apart van die omgewingsproses

Alongside the environmental-authorisation process above, Saldanha Bay Municipality is separately deciding a land-use application covering the same farm portions, lodged in March 2025. Two different planning tools are involved, and it matters which one applies: a “consent use” permits a specific additional use on land that keeps its existing zoning, and can carry conditions or a time limit; a rezoning or “departure” changes the zoning itself, indefinitely, regardless of whether the specific project goes ahead. The municipality has confirmed the land remains zoned Agricultural, and that this application is being handled as a consent use. As of September 2026, the application has not been decided — environmental studies and residents' objections must still be taken into account first.

Saam met die omgewingsmagtigingsproses hierbo, besluit Saldanhabaai Munisipaliteit apart oor 'n grondgebruik-aansoek wat dieselfde plaasgedeeltes dek, in Maart 2025 ingedien. Twee verskillende beplanningsinstrumente is betrokke, en dit maak saak watter een geld: 'n “vergunningsgebruik” laat 'n spesifieke bykomende gebruik toe op grond wat sy bestaande sonering behou, en kan voorwaardes of 'n tydsbeperking dra; 'n hersonering of “afwyking” verander die sonering self, onbepaald, ongeag of die spesifieke projek voortgaan. Die munisipaliteit het bevestig die grond bly Landbou gesoneer, en dat hierdie aansoek as 'n vergunningsgebruik hanteer word. Vanaf September 2026 is nog geen besluit oor die aansoek geneem nie — omgewingstudies en inwoners se besware moet eers steeds in ag geneem word.

Who owns the land, and since when. The four title deeds are bound into the solar Final Scoping Report itself (appendix M2). They show the land was bought outright, not leased, by Cultisol Holdings (Pty) Ltd, in four private sales between August 2023 and May 2024: about 4,735 hectares for R55.3 million, including VAT. The owner has given the solar applicant written permission to develop solar, wind and battery storage on all four (appendix M1). One deed covers a portion of Farm 172 (Buurmans Twist, about 1,482 ha), which the municipal land-use application lists but neither environmental application does.

Wie besit die grond, en sedert wanneer. Die vier titelaktes is ingesluit by die sonkragplaas se Finale Bestekverslag (bylae M2). Dit wys die grond is volledig gekoop, nie gehuur nie, deur Cultisol Holdings (Edms.) Bpk., in vier private transaksies vanaf Augustus 2023 tot Mei 2024: sowat 4 735 hektaar vir R55,3 miljoen, BTW ingesluit. Die eienaar het die sonkragplaas-aansoeker skriftelik toestemming gegee om son-, wind- en batterybergingsprojekte op al vier te ontwikkel (bylae M1). Een akte dek 'n gedeelte van Plaas 172 (Buurmans Twist, sowat 1 482 ha), wat wel die munisipale grondgebruik-aansoek insluit, maar géén van die twee omgewingsaansoeke nie.

Why the fragmentation is a live grievance

Hoekom die verbrokkeling 'n aktuele kwelpunt is

No cumulative assessment. No single study weighs the plant, solar farm, campus, power line, wind facility and biomass plant together. The fuel-plant screening report lists 28 approved or pending wind and solar developments within 30 km (several 8–9 km away); the solar Draft Scoping Report lists about 18 — and confines cumulative impact to “sense of place and visual impacts.” South African law requires cumulative impacts on water, biodiversity, traffic, air and visual amenity to be assessed properly.

Geen kumulatiewe beoordeling nie. Geen enkele studie weeg die aanleg, sonkragplaas, kampus, kraglyn, windkragfasiliteit en biomassa-aanleg saam nie. Die brandstofaanleg se siftingsverslag lys 28 goedgekeurde of hangende wind- en sonprojekte binne 30 km (verskeie 8–9 km weg); die sonkragplaas se Konsep-Bestekverslag lys ongeveer 18 — en beperk kumulatiewe impak tot “gevoel van plek en visuele impakte”. Suid-Afrikaanse wet vereis dat kumulatiewe impakte op water, biodiversiteit, verkeer, lug en visuele omgewing behoorlik geassesseer word.

The applicant's own master plan shows one integrated complex — and it is now in the solar farm's Final Scoping Report itself. That report's section 2.3 layout (August 2026) places the whole facility on one farm: the phased solar array, a battery, a hydrogen plant, methanol synthesis and storage tanks, a carbon-capture unit, a biomass plant and store, a fuel-processing plant, two “safety torch” (flare) zones, an effluent-treatment plant, a truck-loading area and several “future expansion” blocks. This is the same picture as an earlier tender drawing (dated 3 October 2025). Yet the same report still calls the campus a separate application and says “the exact development area will be determined in the EIA phase.”

The fuel-plant consultant’s own answers pull in opposite directions. Asked why no other sites were considered, it repeats the draft report: “Due to the requirement to have the plant in close proximity of the Solar PV and BESS facility, the current site position is the only feasible alternative.” Asked why the plant and solar farm are assessed separately, it answers that “both projects have the ability to operate independently from each other.” The plant cannot be sited elsewhere because it depends on the solar farm, yet it is treated as independent of the solar farm for assessment. Gedokumenteer

No alternatives assessed. The solar Final Scoping Report repeats the solar Draft Scoping Report word for word: “A location alternative would mean reconsidering land tenure options and negotiations with landowners. A location alternative is therefore not considered.” The same sentence appears, verbatim, in the Aquatic Impact Assessment and the Soil & Agricultural statement. The fuel-plant report states that “no other site alternatives are being considered.” South Africa's National Environmental Management Act (section 24(4)(b)) requires a genuine assessment of alternatives, including site alternatives; Heritage Western Cape has also formally required it.

Geen alternatiewe beoordeel nie. Die sonkragplaas se Finale Bestekverslag herhaal die sonkragplaas se Konsep-Bestekverslag woord vir woord: “'n Liggingsalternatief sou beteken dat grondbesitopsies en onderhandelinge met grondeienaars heroorweeg word. 'n Liggingsalternatief word dus nie oorweeg nie.” Dieselfde sin verskyn, woordeliks, in die Akwatiese Impakstudie en die Grond-&-Landbou-verklaring. Die brandstofaanleg-verslag sê dat “geen ander terrein-alternatiewe word oorweeg nie.” Suid-Afrika se Nasionale Wet op Omgewingsbestuur (artikel 24(4)(b)) vereis 'n werklike evaluering van alternatiewe, insluitend terrein-alternatiewe; Erfenis Wes-Kaap het dit ook formeel vereis.

Recycled templates. The government's screening tool vs the reports

Die regering se siftingsinstrument teenoor die verslae

Before an application is lodged, the developer must run the national environmental screening tool — the government's own system. It returns the site's sensitivity ratings and the list of specialist assessments required. This section is a straight comparison of what the tool requires with what the scoping reports propose.

Voordat 'n aansoek ingedien word, moet die ontwikkelaar die nasionale omgewingsiftingstelsel — die regering se eie stelsel — gebruik. Dit lewer die terrein se sensitiwiteitsgraderings en die lys spesialisevaluerings wat vereis word. Hierdie afdeling is 'n lynregte vergelyking van dit wat die stelsel vereis, met wat die bestekverslae voorstel.

The fuel-plant site (61 hectares)

Die brandstofaanleg-terrein (61 hektaar)

The screening tool rates it Very High for Terrestrial Biodiversity, Aquatic Biodiversity and Agriculture, High for Animal Species (naming the Black Harrier and Southern Black Korhaan) and High for Civil Aviation. The tool builds its list of required studies from the type of development the applicant selects when running it. The applicant ran it six times, each under a different development category, all published as part of the plant’s Appendix D. The categories, exactly as printed at the top of each report, are “Infrastructure | Localised infrastructure | Storage | Dangerous Goods | Chemicals”, “Activity requiring permit or licence in terms of National or Provincial legislation governing the release or generation of emissions | Emissions”, “Transformation of land | Indigenous vegetation”, “Transformation of land | From agriculture or afforestation”, “Services | Water services | Treatment and Waste Water” and “Utilities Infrastructure | Pipelines | Water | Waste Water”. The six runs return overlapping lists; between them they require, among others: a Palaeontology assessment (all six runs), a Hydrology assessment (five of six), a Geotechnical assessment (three of six), a Health Impact Assessment and Air Quality assessments (two of six), a Noise assessment (two of six) and a Seismicity assessment (one).

Die siftingstelsel gradeer dit Baie Hoog vir landelike biodiversiteit, akwatiese biodiversiteit en landbou, Hoog vir dierspesies (met vermelding van die Witkruisvleivalk en die Swartvlerkkorhaan) en Hoog vir burgerlugvaart. Die stelsel stel sy lys vereiste studies saam uit die tipe ontwikkeling wat die aansoeker kies wanneer dit geloop word. Die aansoeker het dit ses keer geloop, elke keer onder ’n ander ontwikkelingskategorie, almal gepubliseer as deel van die aanleg se Bylae D. Die kategorieë, presies soos bo-aan elke verslag gedruk (in Engels), is “Infrastructure | Localised infrastructure | Storage | Dangerous Goods | Chemicals”, “Activity requiring permit or licence in terms of National or Provincial legislation governing the release or generation of emissions | Emissions”, “Transformation of land | Indigenous vegetation”, “Transformation of land | From agriculture or afforestation”, “Services | Water services | Treatment and Waste Water” en “Utilities Infrastructure | Pipelines | Water | Waste Water”. Die ses lopies lewer oorvleuelende lyste op; gesamentlik vereis dit onder meer: 'n paleontologiese evaluering (vir al ses lopies), 'n hidrologiese evaluering (vir vyf van die ses), 'n geotegniese evaluering (vir drie van die ses), 'n evaluering van gesondheidsimpak en evaluerings van luggehalte (vir twee van die ses), 'n evaluering vir geraas (vir twee van die ses) en 'n seismisiteitsevaluering (vir een).

The Final Scoping Report then downgrades or omits nearly every one: Agriculture becomes a “Compliance Statement,” Hydrology a “Desktop Geohydrological Assessment,” Noise a “Compliance Statement,” the air-quality studies collapse into one “Atmospheric Emissions Assessment,” and there is no Health Impact Assessment and no Geotechnical Assessment at all — for a chemical plant with a flare, near a town. The palaeontology assessment was waived on a motivation letter. The screening protocol requires the practitioner to motivate every omission “with photographic evidence.”

Since then, the decision-maker has asked for most of them. In written comments on the draft (12 June 2026), the Western Cape environment department — which will decide this application — told the consultant that a Health Impact Assessment and an Ambient Air Quality Impact Assessment “must be undertaken”; that the geotechnical and geohydrological studies “must not be limited to desktop assessments” and must include site-specific investigations and the associated pipelines and roads; that the summary of sensitivities must cover all the screening reports; and that layout, design, operational and “no-go” alternatives must all be assessed, not only site and technology. The consultant accepted each point in writing, so these studies should appear in the Draft Environmental Impact Report. Documented Two gaps remain: none of the six screening runs specifies the actual development footprint, and four of them were generated on 18 June 2026, after public comment on the draft had closed — so the Draft Environmental Impact Report will be the first chance to comment on them.

Die Finale Bestekverslag verlaag of laat dan byna elkeen weg: Landbou word 'n “Nakomingsverklaring,” Hidrologie 'n “Lessenaar-Geohidrologiese Evaluering,” Geraas 'n “Nakomingsverklaring,” die lugkwaliteitstudies val saam in een “Atmosferiese Vrystellings-evaluering,” en daar is glad geen Gesondheidsimpakstudie en geen Geotegniese Evaluering nie — vir 'n chemiese aanleg met 'n fakkel, digby 'n dorp. Die paleontologie-evaluering is op 'n motiveringsbrief kwytgeskeld. Die siftingsprotokol vereis dat die praktisyn elke weglating “met fotografiese bewyse” motiveer.

Sedertdien het die besluitnemer die meeste daarvan aangevra. In skriftelike kommentaar op die konsep (12 Junie 2026) het die Wes-Kaapse omgewingsdepartement — wat oor hierdie aansoek sal besluit — die konsultant meegedeel dat ’n Gesondheidsimpakstudie en ’n Omgewingslugkwaliteit-impakstudie gedoen “moet word”; dat die geotegniese en geohidrologiese studies “nie tot lessenaarstudies beperk mag word nie” en terrein-spesifieke ondersoeke en die gepaardgaande pyplyne en paaie moet insluit; dat die opsomming van sensitiwiteite al die siftingsverslae moet dek; en dat uitleg-, ontwerp-, bedryfs- en “geen-gang”-alternatiewe almal beoordeel moet word, nie net terrein en tegnologie nie. Die konsultant het elke punt skriftelik aanvaar, so hierdie studies behoort in die Konsep-Omgewingsimpakverslag te verskyn. Gedokumenteer Twee leemtes bly: geeneen van die ses siftingslopies spesifiseer die werklike ontwikkelingsvoetspoor nie, en vier daarvan is op 18 Junie 2026 opgestel, nádat openbare kommentaar op die konsep gesluit het — so die Konsep-Omgewingsimpakverslag sal die eerste geleentheid wees om daarop kommentaar te lewer.

The solar site

Die sonkragplaasterrein

The solar screening rates the site Very High for Terrestrial Biodiversity, Aquatic Biodiversity, Agriculture and Landscape; High for Animal Species and Plant Species; and Medium for Civil Aviation, Palaeontology and Defence. Regardless of rating, it identifies 13 required specialist assessments — because the protocol requires the practitioner either to include each identified study or to “motivate… the reason for not including” it “with photographic evidence.”

Die sonkragplaas-sifting gradeer die terrein Baie Hoog vir landelike biodiversiteit, akwatiese biodiversiteit, Landbou en Landskap; Hoog vir Dierspesies en Plantspesies; en Medium vir Burgerlugvaart, Paleontologie en Verdediging. Ongeag gradering, identifiseer dit 13 vereiste spesialis-evaluerings — want die protokol vereis dat die praktisyn óf elke geïdentifiseerde studie insluit óf “die rede vir nie-insluiting motiveer” “met fotografiese bewyse.”

The solar Final Scoping Report proposes seven (socio-economic, biodiversity, visual, a combined heritage / palaeontology study, traffic, soil & agriculture, aquatic), omitting with no motivation the Civil Aviation, Defence, Geotechnical and radio-frequency-interference assessments and the standalone Plant Species and Animal Species assessments. It also enters no development footprint into the tool, so the whole screening runs on the ~3,252-hectare study area.

Die sonkragplaas se Finale Bestekverslag stel sewe voor (sosio-ekonomies, biodiversiteit, visueel, 'n gekombineerde erfenis- / paleontologiestudie, verkeer, grond & landbou, akwaties), en daar is weglatings sonder motivering van die Burgerlugvaart-, Verdediging-, Geotegniese en radiofrekwensie-inmengingsevaluerings en die aparte Plantspesie- en Dierspesie-evaluerings. Dit voer ook geen ontwikkelingsvoetspoor in die stelsel nie, so die hele sifting loop op die ~3 252-hektaar studiegebied.

Civil aviation and glare — the studies disagree with each other

Burgerlugvaart en weerkaatste lig – die studies verskil van mekaar

The screening tool rates civil aviation “High” for the plant and “Medium” for the solar site. The solar farm's own Glare Assessment (September 2024), using standard modelling, finds “yellow” glare — bright enough to cause temporary after-image and rated a “high risk to traffic” — on the R45, Oak Street / Hopefield Road, Voortrekker Street (present more than six months of the year, from about 90% of the site) and other roads, and recommends removing solar arrays from part of Phase 3A entirely. It also finds green glare at the Air Force Base Langebaanweg control tower and along two flight paths.

Die siftingstelsel gradeer burgerlugvaart “Hoog” vir die aanleg en “Medium” vir die sonkragplaasterrein. Die sonkragplaas se eie evaluering van weerkaatsingsglans (September 2024), wat van standaardmodellering gebruik maak, identifiseer “geel” glans – helder genoeg om 'n tydelike nabeeld te veroorsaak en geklassifiseer as 'n “hoë risiko vir verkeer” – op die R45, Oakstraat/Hopefieldweg, Voortrekkerstraat (wat meer as ses maande van die jaar vanaf ongeveer 90% van die terrein sigbaar is) en ander paaie; dit beveel aan dat sonpaneelskikkings uit 'n deel van Fase 3A heeltemal verwyder word. Dit identifiseer ook “groen” glans by die beheertoring van die Lugmagbasis Langebaanweg en langs twee vliegroetes.

The earlier Visual Impact Assessment had dismissed glare in a paragraph — “standard solar module produces less glare and reflectance than standard window glass.” The two studies reach opposite conclusions. Neither the plant nor the solar Final Scoping Report includes a Civil Aviation study, and the solar Draft Scoping Report expressly excludes one while conceding the military flight path is “in close proximity.”

Die vroeëre Visuele Impakstudie het weerkaatsingsglans in een paragraaf afgemaak — “standaard sonmodule produseer minder glans en weerkaatsing as standaard vensterglas.” Die twee studies kom tot teenoorgestelde gevolgtrekkings. Nóg die aanleg s'n, nóg die sonkragplaas se Finale Bestekverslag sluit 'n Burgerlugvaartstudie in, en die sonkragplaas se Konsep-Bestekverslag sluit een uitdruklik uit terwyl dit erken die militêre vlugroete is “in die onmiddellike nabyheid.”

The Air Force Base's own 28 April 2026 letter (see below) states: “glare risk is governed by geometry (alignment with flight paths and sun angles), not distance.”

Die Lugmagbasis se eie brief, gedateer 28 April 2026 (sien hieronder), verklaar: “risiko vir weerkaatsingsglans word deur geometrie bepaal (belyning met vlugpaaie en sonhoeke), nie afstand nie.”

CapeNature — provinsiale bewaringsowerheid

9 July 2026. “CapeNature unfortunately objects to this application.” The site development plan “in its current format is fatally flawed from a biodiversity perspective.” The arrays sit “directly within a landscape macro-ecological corridor” that “cannot be offset”; the “lake effect … on avifauna is expected to be substantial”; and “plant species in vegetation maintained under solar panels will be shaded out, and thus the PV farm areas should be regarded as cleared areas” — which runs against the developer's public argument that fynbos can persist under the panels. Air Force Base Langebaanweg — SA Air Force, Department of Defence

Lugmagbasis Langebaanweg — SA Lugmag, Departement van Verdediging

28 April 2026, to the Saldanha Bay Municipal Manager. Headed “OBJECTION TO THE APPLICATION.” The site is within the Langebaanweg Control Zone — controlled airspace from ground level to 19,500 feet, about 6–7 km from an active runway. Glint and glare is “the most critical risk,” and “glare risk is governed by geometry (alignment with flight paths and sun angles), not distance.” Reflective arrays and electrical infrastructure risk interference with radar, instrument landing systems and navigation. The base wants the project referred to the civil-aviation and air-force obstacle-evaluation processes before any planning approval, a formal glint-and-glare and interference assessment, and an aquifer impact study — and asks expressly that “approval be withheld pending the completion and review of the required aviation and environmental assessments.”

28 April 2026, aan die Saldanhabaai Munisipale Bestuurder. Opskrif “BESWAAR TEEN DIE AANSOEK.” Die terrein is binne die Langebaanweg-beheersone — beheerde lugruim vanaf grondvlak tot 19 500 voet, ongeveer 6–7 km van 'n aktiewe aanloopbaan. Glans en weerkaatsing is “die mees kritieke risiko,” en “weerkaatsingsrisiko word deur geometrie bepaal (belyning met vlugpaaie en sonhoeke), nie afstand nie.” Weerkaatsende skikkings en elektriese infrastruktuur loop die risiko om radar, instrumentlandingstelsels en navigasie te steur. Die basis wil hê die projek moet na die burgerlugvaart- en lugmag-hindernisevaluasieprosesse verwys word voor enige beplanningsgoedkeuring, 'n formele glans en weerkaatsing- en steuringsevaluering, en 'n akwifeer-impakstudie — en vra uitdruklik dat “goedkeuring weerhou word hangende die voltooiing en hersiening van die vereiste lugvaart- en omgewingsevaluerings.”

A larger project downstream: Velddrif

'n Groter projek stroomaf: Velddrif

On 11 February 2026 a German-led renewable-energy group publicly announced a much larger integrated project centred on Velddrif, at the mouth of the Berg River. As announced, it would include about 20 GW of solar and wind in the Northern Cape, a 450 km private transmission-and-pipeline corridor along the existing Sishen–Saldanha railway, a desalination plant at Velddrif scaling to 150 million litres a day — the water split into hydrogen, ammonia and aviation fuels for export through Saldanha Bay — and a new residential development for 150,000 people. The Western Cape Premier was present at the announcement. As of that date the project had begun no environmental, water or regulatory process and there was no signed agreement with the province.

Op 11 Februarie 2026 het 'n Duits-geleide hernubare-energiegroep openlik aangekondig 'n veel groter geïntegreerde projek gesentreer op Velddrif, by die mond van die Bergrivier. Soos aangekondig, sou dit ongeveer 20 GW son en wind in die Noord-Kaap insluit, 'n 450 km private transmissie- en pyplyn-korridor langs die bestaande Sishen–Saldanha-spoorlyn, 'n ontsoutingsaanleg op Velddrif met 'n kapasiteit van 150 miljoen liter per dag — waar die water verdeel word in waterstof, ammoniak en vliegtuigbrandstof vir uitvoer via Saldanhabaai — en 'n nuwe residensiële ontwikkeling vir 150 000 mense. Die Premier van die Wes-Kaap was teenwoordig by die aankondiging. Teen daardie tyd was daar nog geen begin gemaak met enige omgewings-, water- of reguleringsproses vir die projek nie, en daar was geen ondertekende ooreenkoms met die provinsie nie.

Why this matters to Hopefield. Velddrif sits on the Berg Estuary Ramsar Site (declared 2 February 2022, South Africa's 28th wetland of international importance). The fuel-plant scoping report states the Sout River, which runs past Hopefield, is a “tributary of the Berg River” — so the Phelan complex's surface drainage flows to that same Ramsar estuary, while its groundwater pathway reaches a second Ramsar site, Langebaan Lagoon. A 150-million-litre-a-day desalination plant at Velddrif carries its own brine-discharge footprint on that estuary. Two hydrogen-and-desalination complexes now bear on the Berg estuary system and the same port — and no assessment considers them together.

Hoekom dit vir Hopefield saak maak. Velddrif is geleë by die Bergrivier-monding-Ramsar-terrein (verklaar 2 Februarie 2022, Suid-Afrika se 28ste vleiland van internasionale belang). Die brandstofaanleg se bestekverslag verklaar die Soutrivier, wat by Hopefield verby loop, is 'n “sytak van die Bergrivier” — so die Phelan-kompleks se oppervlak-dreinering vloei na daardie selfde Ramsar-mondingsgebied, terwyl sy grondwaterpad 'n tweede Ramsar-terrein bereik, die Langebaan-lagune. 'n 150-miljoen-liter-per-dag ontsoutingsaanleg by Velddrif dra sy eie pekelvrystellingsvoetspoor op daardie mondingsgebied. Twee waterstof-en-ontsoutingskomplekse raak nou die Bergrivier-mondingstelsel en dieselfde hawe — en geen evaluering oorweeg hulle saam nie.

Renewable-energy procurement on the West Coast

Hernubare-energie-verkryging op die Weskus

The municipalities are separately buying renewable power, with the bidder bringing the land and the plant: Saldanha Bay Municipality tender SBM 50/23/24 (about 100 MW, dispatchable, 15 years; closed May 2025); Bergrivier Municipality tender T 8/3/69-2024 (closed April 2025); Swartland Municipality (a solar plant for Darling, and a planned 10 MW plant at Malmesbury); Cederberg Municipality (wind and solar at Clanwilliam and Lambert's Bay). Nationally, the Renewable Energy IPP Procurement Programme continues, and the transmission grid across the Western and Northern Cape is saturated by earlier rounds — the same constraint that means the Phelan solar farm would not connect to the national grid. The Transnet National Ports Authority issued a Request for Information in October 2023 for hydrogen export terminals at seven ports, Saldanha among them.

Die munisipaliteite koop apart hernubare krag, met die tenderaar wat die grond en die aanleg bring: Saldanhabaai Munisipaliteit tender SBM 50/23/24 (ongeveer 100 MW, versendbaar, 15 jaar; gesluit Mei 2025); Bergrivier Munisipaliteit tender T 8/3/69-2024 (gesluit April 2025); Swartland Munisipaliteit ('n sonaanleg vir Darling, en 'n beplande 10 MW aanleg by Malmesbury); Cederberg Munisipaliteit (wind en son by Clanwilliam en Lambertsbaai). Nasionaal duur die Hernubare Energie OKP-Verkrygingsprogram voort, en die transmissienet oor die Wes- en Noord-Kaap is deur vroeëre rondtes versadig — dieselfde beperking wat beteken die Phelan-sonkragplaas sou nie aan die nasionale kragnet koppel nie. Die Transnet Nasionale Hawe-owerheid het in Oktober 2023 'n Versoek om Inligting uitgereik vir waterstof-uitvoerterminale by sewe hawens, Saldanha onder hulle.

Why this belongs in the assessment

Hoekom dit in die evaluering hoort

Intense competition for limited grid capacity, suitable land, water and port access on this coast is direct evidence against the scoping report's claim that the Phelan site is “the only feasible alternative” — and it is exactly the material a proper cumulative-impact assessment would have to weigh, especially for the Berg estuary, now facing two desalination and industrial complexes.

Intense mededinging om beperkte kragnet-kapasiteit, geskikte grond, water en hawe-toegang op hierdie kus is direkte bewys teen die bestekverslag se bewering dat die Phelan-terrein “die enigste uitvoerbare alternatief” is — en dit is presies die materiaal wat 'n behoorlike kumulatiewe-impak-evaluering sou moes weeg, veral vir die Bergrivier-mondingsgebied, wat nou twee ontsoutings- en nywerheidskomplekse in die gesig staar.

Jobs · economyThe employment figures are the least substantiated part of the project Claimed

What is claimed

About 2,500 construction jobs over roughly five years; about 500 permanent operational jobs. The Final Scoping Report rates “creation of employment” as a positive impact of high significance — but its Social Impact Assessment has no Hopefield-specific data: “Data specific to Ward 7 within Hopefield will be included once interviews and consultations… are concluded.” Peak construction is about 400 workers a day, “transported from the surrounding areas.”

The assessment undermines its own numbers

The Social Impact Assessment traces the job figures to a renewable-energy news website — not to the applicant or a labour study; the article it cites is a piece questioning green-hydrogen viability. The assessment records that “a rumour was noted during consultation that approximately 2,500 workers would be brought in during construction” — treating its own headline number as a rumour — and describes the 500 permanent roles as work in “hydrogen production, engineering, plant operations” requiring “a new skilled labour market in green hydrogen,” which is not what solar operation needs and not what a farmworker town is positioned to fill.

The numbers move, and the one specialist estimate is small

The January 2025 baseline report contradicts itself — one section says “2000 jobs over … 5 years and more than 3000 indirect jobs,” another repeats “2,500 jobs” over four years. The only figure from an actual specialist calculation is in the solar traffic study, which models a permanent operational workforce of about eight people per 150 MW phase. At the May 2026 public meeting the consultant put peak construction at about 1,000, not 2,500, with 2,500 a cumulative-over-time figure.

The baseline the company published works against its own claim

The published Saldanha-Bay figures: about 11% of adults have any post-school qualification; 40% did not complete secondary school. A chemical plant making hydrogen and jet fuel needs post-school engineering and technical skills; the company has not disclosed the skill or wage profile of the jobs despite making them the headline benefit; and agriculture — the sector being displaced — provides about 38% of district employment. The assessment also never contemplates a “no-go” outcome, only “mitigation.”

Gains must be netted against losses

Every job figure quoted is a gross number. No one has assessed the jobs the project puts at risk in the existing Hopefield economy — apiculture and honey manufacture, wildflower cultivation and the Fynbos Show, small-scale farming, domestic employment, local building trades, and town-based businesses (see The industries that depend on the fynbos).

What the record shows would make it real

A Social Impact Assessment redone with actual fieldwork in Hopefield (see the baseline); a breakdown of the jobs claim by skill and by local-versus-imported, sourced to the applicant; numeric local-employment and local-procurement targets by phase as licence conditions; a funded skills pipeline started before construction; a community equity stake or trust with a stated percentage and trustworthy governance; and public advertising of every project post.

Economy · delivery riskThe project could clear the land and then stall Contested

Documented

The commercial basis is the EU and UK synthetic-fuel mandates, which are real and undersupplied. But no commercial-scale synthetic-jet-fuel project anywhere had reached a final investment decision as of mid-2026; these plants need government price guarantees and airlines under firm contract before a bank will lend. The product has changed three times in three years, and the 2023 status was granted for a “Green Ammonia Project.” The report also makes the carbon-dioxide supply — and therefore the whole plant — dependent on a third-party biomass combustion facility that is not in any visible application.

Uncertain

Whether the developer has signed buyers and financing for the Hopefield plant, for which product; who builds and runs the biomass plant, where, and on what feedstock; whether Phase 1 proceeds on schedule; the fallback if the economics fail.

Why this matters to the town

The worst realistic outcome is not a dangerous product left behind — the finished fuel is ordinary jet fuel and could be sold to any airline. It is ecological damage and disruption locked in by an early authorisation, with the investment and jobs arriving late, small, or not at all.

What the record shows would reduce it

Staged, legally binding conditions: no vegetation clearing until financing and signed fuel-purchase contracts are demonstrated; milestone deadlines with lapse provisions; a rehabilitation guarantee that applies on abandonment at any stage; and disclosure of the fuel-purchase contracts and any government price-support position before authorisation.

Local economy · what is already at riskThe jobs the project could put at risk have never been counted Contested

What Hopefield's economy is

Beyond wheat and sheep, the town runs on things the project threatens. Three established local industries depend on the fynbos itself — honey, cut-flowers and nursery stock, and wildflower tourism (see their own section): between them, a commercial flower farm and the honey businesses alone employ well over 200 people, mostly local, permanent and seasonal. On top of that: small-scale and community farming, domestic employment, local building and trade small businesses, and town-based light manufacturing and services with customers across the Western Cape.

What the assessment says

The Social Impact Assessment concedes the project would displace “informal or land-based livelihoods,” “small-scale or informal farming,” and open space used “for cultural or social purposes” — rated High negative before mitigation — but contains no census of who is affected and offers only “fair compensation” with no mechanism.

Property values

Peer-reviewed work finds housing within about 4 km of a hydrogen production facility loses about 4–5% of value; homes near refineries lose more. Hopefield sits about 1–3 km from the plant. Falling property values erode household wealth and, in turn, demand for domestic workers and local trades.

What the record shows would reduce it

A baseline that quantifies at-risk employment and nets losses against verifiable gains; an affected-persons census and a livelihood-restoration framework (which the international standards the assessment itself invokes require); specific mitigation for the apiculture and wildflower industries; and a property-value protection or compensation mechanism for the households closest to the plant.

Water · groundwaterThe scoping report rates this “high,” cites a pathway to Langebaan Lagoon — then proposes a desktop study Documented

What the report itself says

The plant sits about 875 m from the Sout River. The site overlies a “highly permeable” aquifer with “high” vulnerability to contamination. The report states plainly: groundwater from the property “would drain towards the Langebaan Lagoon” about 29 km south-west, and the lagoon's ecosystems “are dependent on groundwater flows,” so “it is vital that the aquifer be protected.” It rates two impacts — contamination from spills or leaks, and contaminated groundwater migrating “ultimately [to] the Langebaan Lagoon” — as HIGH significance before mitigation.

The problem

The screening tool requires a “Hydrology Assessment.” The report proposes only a “Desktop Geohydrological Assessment” — and the groundwater input notes that “no site visit was undertaken.” The department deciding the application has since required site-specific investigations instead, and the consultant has agreed (see the screening section). Water demand is about 8 million litres a day from the town's drinking-water line, in a catchment where groundwater is “the only readily available source of water” outside town. Brines and salts are trucked off-site, so it is not accurate to say that the plant will generate “zero liquid discharge.” One on-site borehole already shows salinity above the guideline, and downgradient boreholes show trace-metal and nitrate exceedances.

The water story keeps changing (see What is on the record): the developer's May 2025 letter promises “we will not use water from the Saldanha Municipality town supply,” which contradicts the scoping report's 8 million litres a day from the Hopefield line. The notes of the March 2025 meeting record residents asking for the water plan “in writing” — many months on it still does not exist in a single consistent form.

The Berg Estuary Ramsar Site now faces two projects at once (see Other projects on the same coast). The Air Force Base's April 2026 letter also flags the aquifer — “long-term groundwater vulnerability” — and asks for “an environmental impact study … to determine the impact of this large scale operation on the aquifer system.”

What the record shows would reduce it

A field-based hydrogeological investigation and a calibrated numerical groundwater model, independently peer-reviewed and public before authorisation — not a desktop study; desalination as the primary water source from the start; a legal guarantee that Hopefield's drinking-water supply is never reduced or reprioritised; full containment with a monitoring-borehole network and public quarterly data.

Air · dust · fireDust, flares and fire risk next to the town Documented

What is at stake

Construction dust from clearing and grading on sandy, wind-exposed ground — the scoping report itself flags an “Exceedance of NAAQS” (national air-quality standards) as a construction impact. For operations it concedes the plant “exceeds NAAQS beyond the facility boundary where there is public access” (though not, it argues, at the nearest homes). Routine and flare emissions from the fuel plant, which combusts methane, hydrogen and carbon monoxide. Fire and explosion risk from the hazardous inventory. Over the solar farm, loss of vegetation and soil crust over thousands of hectares changes local wind and dust generation.

On the “heat” fear specifically

Large solar farms do warm the air directly above the panels by about 1–4 °C, but that effect stays local to the array and fades within tens of metres of its edge — it will not measurably heat a town 1 km away. The real local effect is indirect: stripping vegetation off thousands of hectares. The evidence is strong on dust, fire and vegetation loss.

What the screening tool requires — and the report skips

The screening for the emissions licence requires a Health Impact Assessment, an Ambient Air Quality Impact Assessment and an Air Quality Impact Assessment. The report proposes a single “Atmospheric Emissions Assessment” and no Health Impact Assessment at all. The department deciding the application has since required both a Health Impact Assessment and an Ambient Air Quality Impact Assessment, and the consultant has confirmed in writing that both will be done.

What the record shows would reduce it

A full Health Impact Assessment and both air-quality studies; enforceable dust-management and air-quality monitoring with public data and stop-work triggers; retention of ground cover between panel rows; a vegetated buffer between the array and town; an independent fire risk assessment and a funded joint fire-response arrangement; and binding limits and monitoring in the Atmospheric Emission Licence.

Wat die verslag self sê

Die aanleg lê ongeveer 875 m van die Soutrivier. Die terrein lê oor 'n “hoogs deurlaatbare” akwifeer met “hoë” kwesbaarheid vir besoedeling. Die verslag stel dit eenvoudig: grondwater van die eiendom “sou na die Langebaan-lagune afloop” ongeveer 29 km suidwes, en die lagune se ekostelsels “is afhanklik van grondwatervloei,” so “dit is noodsaaklik dat die akwifeer beskerm word.” Dit gradeer twee impakte — besoedeling deur stortings of lekkasies, en besoedelde grondwater wat migreer “uiteindelik [na] die Langebaan-lagune” — as HOË betekenis voor versagting.

Die probleem

Die siftingstelsel vereis 'n “Hidrologiese Evaluering.” Die verslag stel net 'n “Lessenaar-Geohidrologiese Evaluering” voor — en die grondwater-inset merk op dat “geen terreinbesoek onderneem is nie.” Die departement wat oor die aansoek besluit, het sedertdien eerder terrein-spesifieke ondersoeke vereis, en die konsultant het ingestem (sien die siftingsafdeling). Watervraag is ongeveer 8 miljoen liter per dag van die dorp se drinkwaterlyn, in 'n opvanggebied waar grondwater “die enigste geredelik beskikbare waterbron” buite die dorp is. Land · biodiversityIndependent specialists and the government tool both rate the site as highly sensitive Documented

On the record

Independent botanists report an unusually high concentration of threatened plant species across the area — roughly 25 threatened species — in Hopefield Sand Fynbos (Vulnerable) and, to the east, Swartland Shale Renosterveld (Critically Endangered). More than 30% of the site is mapped as the province's highest-protection category (Critical Biodiversity Area — terrestrial, wetland and river), plus Ecological Support Area; the West Coast National Park is within 5–10 km. The government screening tool rates both the plant and the solar site “Very High” for terrestrial and aquatic biodiversity and “High” for animal species, and the solar screening names the species it flags: the Southern Black Korhaan and Black Harrier, plus the African Marsh Harrier, Secretarybird, Tawny Eagle, a golden mole and a threatened dung beetle, and about thirty plant species. The fuel-plant report's own biodiversity maps show the eastern part of the footprint, and much of its regulated buffer, inside the highest terrestrial category, with the Sout River mapped as a river Critical Biodiversity Area flanked by a wide corridor.

The field survey recorded threatened plants on site — then contradicted itself

The developer's biodiversity report (February 2024) lists, at section 5.1.6, eight species of conservation concern “encountered within the study site” — including the Endangered Hopefield Cornbush and Snakestem Pincushion. Its section 8 conclusion then says the survey was “unproductive with regards to finding plants of conservation significance” and re-lists the same eight species as merely “high potential of occurrence.” Body says found; conclusion says not found — and it is the conclusion's wording the scoping reports carry forward. The report's “no Red Data species recorded” line refers only to fauna and birds.

Why the biodiversity basis is weak

Both applications rest on one biodiversity study — a spring-only, daytime-only survey over 1–2 days in November 2023, by a firm based in another province. The specialist's own words: “the assessment was based on one day's dry season (spring) survey only… Site visits should ideally be conducted over differing seasons.” No night surveys, despite bats and owls being noted. The report also concedes that the mandatory site-sensitivity verification “was not readily available to the specialist at the time.” And it is a recycled template from a mining assessment: the mitigation text repeatedly refers to “mining activities,” “waste from blasting or excavations,” and elsewhere to “the proposed housing development.” Residents identified both problems — the April 2025 comments record notes that the survey was done in the dry season with plants dormant, and for a facility of a different size.

The two flagship birds were filtered out

The report assesses only six Red Data bird species and states that species “with a reporting rate of [less than] 1% were not included in the study.” That filter drops the Black Harrier (Endangered) and the Southern Black Korhaan (Vulnerable) — both Cape endemics, both in the report's own 181-species desktop list, and both flagged as “High” for this site by the government screening tool. Even so, the report rates the impact on birds “Very High” before mitigation — on its own scale, above the level it says “may constitute a fatal flaw” — then brings it to “medium” on paper with diverters and buffers. A Black Harrier specialist has since been appointed, with fieldwork scheduled for 14 September 2026, and CapeNature-requested surveys for 7 September — both only around the time the Final Scoping Report was circulated on 8 September, not before it.

CapeNature has objected outright

Its 9 July 2026 letter states that it “objects to this application” and that the site plan “in its current format is fatally flawed from a biodiversity perspective” (see Government bodies that have objected). It requires a spring survey, renosterveld confirmation, nesting and breeding screening, a standalone aquatic impact assessment, a reduced site plan, and assessment together with the fuel-plant application. None of that is in the Final Scoping Report.

Can fynbos survive under the panels?

This is the developer's on-the-record answer to the biodiversity objection: its comments-and-responses says “minimal vegetation clearance … the roots and seed bank is retained … vegetation regrowth happens naturally.” The claim is borrowed from temperate-grassland agrivoltaics, where light shade and no herbicide can support reasonable biodiversity. It does not transfer to fynbos and renosterveld, mainly because of fire: these systems must burn every 10–15 years to regenerate, and you cannot burn under an array — so over one panel lifetime the plant community simplifies regardless. Deep shade (most fynbos shrubs need full sun), soil disturbance and altered rainfall add to that. CapeNature reached the same conclusion: vegetation under the panels “will be shaded out,” and “the PV farm areas should be regarded as cleared areas.”

The biodiversity study exists in two versions

The copy residents first reviewed opens: “The proponent intends to establish a 900MW … Solar Energy Facility.” The copy now bound into the solar Final Scoping Report reads “a 1.6GW … Solar Energy Facility” — same report number, same date, same version label, headline capacity changed with no revision. The underlying one-day survey did not change with it.

Still uncertain

The full species inventory (a single spring survey cannot give it); the extirpation risk for the Hopefield-endemic Cornbush; whether credible offsets exist. A dedicated Aquatic Impact Assessment now exists (wet-season, July 2026) — a credentialed study that confirms the “Very High” rating and delineates six wetlands including the Sout River floodplain wetland — but its “Low impact” conclusion is expressly conditional on “the current layout” avoiding every wetland and buffer, while the report says the footprint is not yet decided; and it is a surface-water study only — it does not assess groundwater, so the plant's Langebaan-aquifer pathway is still assessed nowhere.

What the record shows would reduce it

The change most often named on the record is relocating the solar array onto already-transformed cropland — an independent botanist's phrase is “move this thing 20 km to the east… and you've got no environmental issues,” and one of the developer's own cited sources puts about 13% of the municipality in a degraded state. Failing relocation: hard no-go zones around Critical Biodiversity Areas and wetlands, a search-and-rescue and rehabilitation programme with a financial guarantee, a multi-season survey by a Cape-flora botanist that resolves the report's own contradiction, and independent botanical review.

Heritage · deep time · living culture“Heritage” has been reduced to four farm buildings — and the regulator has already overruled the “low” finding Documented

The regulator has already overruled the “low” finding

The fuel-plant report reports “no heritage,” and the solar Draft Scoping Report rates heritage “low.” But Heritage Western Cape, after a 2024 meeting, formally responded that “there is reason to believe that the proposed PV Solar energy facility … will impact on heritage resources” and requires a full Heritage Impact Assessment meeting section 38(3) of the National Heritage Resources Act — including a dedicated Archaeological Impact Assessment, an assessment of impact relative to the development's benefits, the results of consultation with affected communities, and consideration of alternatives.

The solar Final Scoping Report has been circulated with none of that — its “heritage study” is the rejected fuel-plant study, a Phase 1 assessment from January 2025 for a different project (“green hydrogen and green ammonia processing facility”), concluding “no heritage resources were identified.” It contains no dedicated archaeological assessment, no assessment of alternatives, no record of community consultation, and does not engage the regulator's requirements. Eighteen months on, there is still no compliant section-38(3) heritage assessment for the solar farm.

Heritage is being defined too narrowly

The whole exercise has come down to four farm buildings from the last 120 years. The National Heritage Resources Act covers — regardless of age — palaeontological and geological sites, archaeological material, graves, cultural landscapes, and “living heritage”: oral tradition, indigenous knowledge systems, and the holistic relationship to nature. This landscape carries all of it:

  • Deep human history. Elandsfontein, about 10 km west of Hopefield, is where the “Saldanha Man” cranium was found in 1953 — an archaic human on the order of half a million years old — alongside one of southern Africa's largest Stone Age and Pleistocene-fauna assemblages.
  • A world-class fossil record next door. The West Coast Fossil Park at Langebaanweg is one of the richest 5-million-year-old fossil sites on Earth.
  • First Nations who are still here. This is Cochoqua (Khoekhoe) ancestral land — the farm's own name, Portugeesche Fontyn, comes from a Khoi clan, and the heritage report's own background section describes the Cochoqua as the people who held this coast at Dutch contact. An assessment that treats them as absent erases a living people and a living knowledge system.

A First Nations council has formally objected — and was not consulted

The Aikonese Cochoqua Khoi Tribal Council — registered by Heritage Western Cape as a Heritage Conservation Body in November 2024, which gives its comments formal standing in any section-38 process — has officially submitted a comment contesting the absence of consultation with the Cochoqua on a large development on their ancestral land, and calling for the process to be suspended and public participation reopened. (Another body, the Western Cape First Nations Collective, has reportedly said the process was inclusive of Khoi-San voices; both positions belong on the record.)

The palaeontology exemption does not hold up

A palaeontological assessment for the solar site was waived on a June 2024 motivation letter. It has four problems: the government's own screening rates palaeontological sensitivity “Medium,” not “Low,” and lists the assessment as required; it covers only the solar farm, not the far deeper fuel-plant excavations; it rests on a claim that the local sands “do not preserve fossils” that Elandsfontein — in exactly those sands, about 10 km away — disproves; and it describes the wrong project (“an Ammonia production facility … about 30 km to the northwest”).

What the record shows would reduce it

The full section-38(3) Heritage Impact Assessment that Heritage Western Cape has already required — both footprints, the buildings, the fossil and archaeological record of all ages, the Khoi cultural landscape, alternatives — from Cape, not other-province, material; the palaeontology exemption revisited to cover the deeper plant excavations and corrected for the project description; and direct, documented consultation with the registered Cochoqua conservation body, on the record.

Dit is die ontwikkelaar se openlike antwoord op die biodiversiteitsbeswaar: die kommentaar-en-antwoorde lui: “minimale plantegroei-opruiming … die wortels en saadbank word behou … plantegroei-hergroei vind natuurlik plaas.” Die bewering is geleen van gematigde-grasveld-agrivoltaïese energie, waar ligte skaduwee en geen onkruiddoder redelike biodiversiteit kan ondersteun. RealThe national strategic case is genuine, not invented

South Africa's own strategy documents — the Hydrogen Society Roadmap (2021), the Green Hydrogen Commercialisation Strategy (2022), and the Just Energy Transition Investment Plan (2023–2027) — treat green hydrogen as a national priority, projected to cut 10–15% of the country's domestic emissions and create up to 360,000 jobs nationally by 2050. This is the underlying policy case for the industry, independent of how this specific project is sited or run.

RealPhelan Green's own delivery record elsewhere

At its existing De Aar and Loeriesfontein solar plants in the Northern Cape, more than 90% of the roughly 2,000 construction jobs reportedly went to local and previously disadvantaged workers, and the company is reported to fund tens of millions of rand a year in community programmes there — a training centre, free town WiFi, an arts centre, healthcare and education support, cited in industry and PR coverage. This doesn't bind what happens at Hopefield, but it is independently reported evidence the company can deliver community benefit at scale elsewhere when it commits to it.

Even discounted heavily, a multi-billion-rand build over years brings real spending on accommodation, services, transport, catering and local trades. RealProfile, skills and diversification

A flagship project raises the area's profile and can seed a local renewables and industrial skills base and supplier ecosystem beyond this one developer — if the training and small-business support are structured to outlast construction.

ConditionalLasting employment for local people

Possible only with a training pipeline started years ahead and enforceable local-hire targets. Without a binding community-benefit agreement, this is marketing.

Nationally, South Africa's renewable-energy programme's community trusts average a 9–12% equity stake, and qualifying communities are projected to share R27.1 billion in dividends over the 20-year life of committed projects — a real, working mechanism, not a hypothetical one; independent reviews also find implementation is uneven, with trusts sometimes compliance-driven rather than community-led, and payouts slow to reach residents. Foundations first

Grondslae eerste

The economic baseline the assessment still owes Hopefield

Die ekonomiese basislyn die evaluering steeds aan Hopefield verskuldig

Before anyone can honestly weigh this project, the missing piece is a proper economic baseline for the town itself. The Social Impact Assessment has none — it extrapolates from Saldanha-Bay-wide statistics and promises Ward 7 data “once consultations are concluded,” which many months later had still not happened. Every job argument, on both sides, is currently a guess. This is the specification a credible assessment must contain.

Voordat enigiemand hierdie projek eerlik kan opweeg, is die ontbrekende stuk 'n behoorlike ekonomiese basislyn vir die dorp self. Die Sosiale Impakstudie beskik nie daaroor nie — dit ekstrapoleer uit Saldanhabaai-wye statistiek en belowe Wyk 7-data “sodra konsultasies afgehandel is,” iets wat baie maande later steeds nie gebeur het nie. Elke argument oor werksgeleenthede, aan albei kante, berus tans op blote raaiwerk. Dit is die vereiste waaraan 'n geloofwaardige evaluering moet voldoen.

1 · Employment profile of Hopefield residents

How many working-age residents are employed, unemployed or not economically active; by age, sex and education; formal versus informal. Crucially — where they work: in Hopefield, or commuting daily to Vredenburg, Saldanha and beyond; in what sectors; for what kind of employer. This is the only honest denominator against which “jobs created in Hopefield” can be measured.

2 · The local businesses the project puts at risk — with headcounts

A census, not an estimate, of employment in: apiculture and honey-product manufacture; wildflower cultivation and the Fynbos Show economy; small-scale and community farming; domestic employment; local building and trade small businesses; and town-based businesses serving customers across the wider Western Cape.

3 · Skills and education profile of the town

A Hopefield-specific breakdown of qualifications, trades and experience — so there is a factual basis for how many of the construction and permanent roles residents could actually fill, and what a training pipeline would have to bridge.

4 · The property market

Recent sale volumes, prices and time-on-market — to establish whether the local market has been affected, and to quantify the second-round effect on domestic and trade employment.

5 · Method

A genuine household and business survey, not a desktop extrapolation; by an independent specialist with no role on the assessment team; with indicators defined up front so the same measures can be re-run after construction and after operation, to verify actual local job gains against this baseline and net them against the losses in point 2.

1 · Indiensnemingsprofiel van Hopefield-inwoners

Hoeveel inwoners van werkende ouderdom in diens, werkloos of nie ekonomies aktief is nie; volgens ouderdom, geslag en onderrig; formeel teenoor informeel. Krities — waar hulle werk: in Hopefield, of daagliks reis na Vredenburg, Saldanha en verder; in watter sektore; vir watter soort werkgewer. Dit is die enigste eerlike noemer waarteen “werk geskep in Hopefield” gemeet kan word.

2 · Die plaaslike besighede wat die projek op die spel plaas — met koptelling

'n Sensus, nie 'n beraming nie, van indiensneming in: byeteelt en heuningproduk-vervaardiging; veldblom-verbouing en die Fynbosskou-ekonomie; kleinskaalse en gemeenskapsboerdery; huishoudelike indiensneming; plaaslike bou- en handel-kleinbesighede; en dorpsgebaseerde besighede wat kliënte regoor die breër Wes-Kaap bedien.

3 · Vaardigheids- en onderrigprofiel van die dorp

'n Hopefield-spesifieke uiteensetting van kwalifikasies, ambagte en ervaring — sodat daar 'n feitelike grondslag is vir hoeveel van die konstruksie- en permanente poste inwoners werklik kon vul, en wat 'n opleidingspyplyn sou moes oorbrug.

4 · Die eiendomsmark

Onlangse verkoopsvolumes, pryse en tyd-op-die-mark — om vas te stel of die plaaslike mark geraak is, en om die tweede-rondte-effek op huishoudelike en handel-indiensneming te kwantifiseer.

5 · Metode

'n Opregte huishoudelike en besigheidsopname, nie 'n lessenaar-ekstrapolasie nie; deur 'n onafhanklike spesialis met geen rol op die evalueringspan nie; met aanwysers vooraf gedefinieer sodat dieselfde maatstawwe ná konstruksie en ná bedryf herloop kan word, om werklike plaaslike werkswins teen hierdie basislyn te verifieer en teen die verliese in punt 2 op te weeg.

Until this exists, the position the record supports on the jobs question is not “the project will” or “won't” help Hopefield — it is that the work to know has not been done.

Totdat dit die geval is, is die standpunt wat deur die beskikbare inligting oor die werkkwessie ondersteun word, nie of die projek Hopefield sal help of nie sal help nie – maar wel dat die nodige werk om dit te bepaal, nog nie gedoen is nie.

Jobs on the other side of the ledger

Werk aan die ander kant van die balansskaal

The industries that depend on the fynbos

Die nywerhede wat van die fynbos afhanklik is

Three established industries in and around Hopefield are built directly on healthy fynbos, clean air and stable groundwater — across the land the project would clear, and downwind and downgradient of the plant. They employ people the project's permanent roles will not: local, permanent and seasonal. None of this is assessed in the Social Impact Assessment (desktop, no site visit) or the biodiversity report (one spring day).

Drie gevestigde bedrywe in en om Hopefield is direk op gesonde fynbos, skoon lug en stabiele grondwater gebou — oor die grond wat die projek sou stroop, en stroomaf en windaf van die aanleg. Hulle stel mense aan wat die projek se permanente poste nie sal nie: plaaslik, permanent en seisoenaal. Niks hiervan word in die Sosiale Impakstudie (lessenaar, geen terreinbesoek) of die biodiversiteitsverslag (een lentedag) beoordeel nie.

Cut-flowers and nursery stock

Snyblomme en kwekery-voorraad

A commercial fynbos cut-flower and nursery operation on the edge of Hopefield works more than 60 hectares and employs about 110 permanent staff, rising to roughly 190 in the harvest season — one of the largest private employers in the town, and the jobs are local. It is exposed to dust and particulate deposition from years of construction and from plant operations, settling on flower crops and young nursery stock (cosmetic damage makes graded flowers unsaleable); to competition for water and any deterioration in groundwater quality; and to the loss of the surrounding veld, which is both a working genetic resource for the industry and part of the Cape-flora provenance it trades on.

'n Kommersiële fynbos-snyblom- en kwekerybedryf op die rand van Hopefield bewerk meer as 60 hektaar en stel ongeveer 110 permanente personeel aan, wat tot ongeveer 190 in die oesseisoen styg — dit is een van die grootste private werkgewers in die dorp, en die werk is plaaslik. Dit word blootgestel aan stof en die neerslag van deeltjies as gevolg van jare se konstruksiewerk en aanlegbedrywighede, wat op blomgewasse en jong kwekeryplante neersak (kosmetiese skade maak gegradeerde blomme onverkoopbaar); aan mededinging om water en enige agteruitgang in die gehalte van grondwater; en aan die verlies van die omliggende veld, wat beide 'n werkende genetiese hulpbron vir die bedryf is en deel van die Kaapse-flora-herkoms waarop dit handel dryf.

Honey and related products

Heuning en heuningprodukte

Hopefield markets itself on fynbos honey. A long-established family beekeeping business in the town employs about 11 local people, and other beekeepers add to that. Bees forage over several kilometres, so clearing 3,000-plus hectares of flowering veld, plus construction and operational dust, directly reduces forage and can contaminate hives — and an industrial neighbour works against a product sold on being “West Coast” and “raw fynbos.”

Hopefield bemark homself op fynbos-heuning. 'n Lank-gevestigde familie-byeteeltbesigheid in die dorp stel ongeveer 11 plaaslike mense aan, en ander byeboere voeg daarby. Bye soek kos oor 'n afstand van verskeie kilometers; die verwydering van meer as 3 000 hektaar blomryke veld – tesame met stof van konstruksie en bedryfsaktiwiteite – verminder dus die beskikbare voedselbronne direk en kan byekorwe besoedel. Daarbenewens werk die teenwoordigheid van 'n nywerheidsbuurman teen 'n produk wat juis bemark word as afkomstig van die “Weskus” en as “rou fynbos”.

Wildflowers and the Fynbos Show economy

Veldblomme en die Fynbosskou-ekonomie

Spring wildflower season and the Hopefield Fynbos Show — the town's flagship annual event, drawing up to 8,000 visitors — support local accommodation, food and retail. Its specimen exhibit, built each year by local botanists, is in effect an annual field inventory of the veld now proposed for clearing. The scoping report's own visual assessment rates sense-of-place impact “High” at points before mitigation.

Lente-veldblomseisoen en die Hopefield Fynbosskou — die dorp se vlagskip jaarlikse geleentheid, wat tot 8 000 besoekers trek — ondersteun plaaslike verblyf, kos en kleinhandel. Die spesie-uitstalling, elke jaar deur plaaslike plantkundiges gebou, is in effek 'n jaarlikse veld-inventaris van die veld wat nou vir stroping voorgestel word. Die bestekverslag se eie visuele evaluasie gradeer plek-gevoel-impak “Hoog” by punte voor versagting.

The common thread: all three industries depend on the same ecological services — pollinator habitat, clean air, groundwater, intact veld and rural sense of place — that the biodiversity, air, water and visual concerns each flag as under threat. They are also, unlike the plant's permanent roles, employment a farmworker town can hold. A credible assessment would treat them as a cluster, with a proper agricultural and horticultural impact assessment of dust and deposition, enforceable dust and air limits with monitoring, a no-go buffer, and formal consultation with the fynbos cut-flower and beekeeping industry bodies.

Die gemene deler: al drie bedrywe is afhanklik van dieselfde ekologiese dienste – habitat vir bestuiwers, skoon lug, grondwater, ongeskonde veld en 'n landelike gevoel van plek – wat deur kwessies rakende biodiversiteit, lug, water en visuele aspekte as bedreig aangedui word. Anders as die aanleg se permanente poste, bied hulle ook werksgeleenthede vir 'n plaaswerkerdorp. 'n Geloofwaardige beoordeling sou hulle as 'n groep hanteer, met 'n behoorlike landbou- en tuinboukundige impakstudie oor stof en neerslag, afdwingbare perke vir stof en luggehalte gepaardgaande met monitering, 'n buffersone waar geen ontwikkeling toegelaat word nie, en formele konsultasie met die bedryfsliggame vir fynbos-snyblomme en byeboerdery.

Documents not yet public

Dokumente nog nie openbaar beskikbaar nie

  • The Major Hazard Installation risk assessment — blast, fire and toxic-release contours and the safe distance to town. What is published as the plant's “Appendix D-1” is only the government screening report.
  • The air-quality / atmospheric-emissions report with modelled ground-level concentrations.
  • The full section-38(3) heritage study Heritage Western Cape has required, and a palaeontological assessment covering the fuel-plant footprint.
  • The full Agricultural Impact Assessment the “Very High” rating requires, and the Department of Agriculture's promised further comment once a layout exists.
  • A properly compiled, per-commenter Comments and Responses report and a corrected “Comments Received” register from Eco-Compliance (the solar farm). WSP’s record for the fuel plant does list every comment individually, with dates and responses, though comments sent through an online comment platform are attributed to the platform rather than to the people who wrote them. The two separate EIA processes’ records should not be conflated.
  • The hydrogen / methanol campus, desalination-plant, wind-facility and 132 kV power-line applications — not yet started.
  • Any application for the biomass plant. The operator (Ithemba BioEnergy), feedstock and scale have now been stated; its location, where some 200,000 tonnes a year of biomass would be sourced, and its own environmental application are still not public. The “green” claim and the plant's carbon-dioxide supply depend on it.
  • The Water Use Licence, Atmospheric Emission Licence and waste management licence applications.
  • Any agreements, incentives or fuel-purchase arrangements between the developer and the province, the municipality or state agencies — obtainable by formal records request.
  • Die risikobeoordeling vir installasies met groot gevare — kontoere vir ontploffings, brande en die vrystelling van giftige stowwe, asook die veilige afstand na die dorp. Wat as die aanleg se “Bylae D-1” gepubliseer word, is net die regering se siftingsverslag.
  • Die verslag oor luggehalte / atmosferiese vrystellings met gemodelleerde konsentrasies op grondvlak.
  • Die volledige erfenisstudie ingevolge artikel 38(3) wat deur Erfenis Wes-Kaap vereis is, en 'n paleontologiese evaluering wat die voetspoor van die brandstofaanleg dek.
  • Die volledige landbou-impakevaluering wat deur die “Baie Hoog”-gradering vereis word, en die Departement van Landbou se beloofde verdere kommentaar sodra 'n uitlegplan beskikbaar is.
  • 'n Behoorlik saamgestelde verslag van kommentaar en reaksies (per kommentaarleweraar) en 'n gekorrigeerde register van “Ontvange Kommentaar” van Eco-Compliance (die sonkragplaas). WSP se rekord vir die brandstofaanleg lys wel elke kommentaar afsonderlik, met datums en reaksies, alhoewel kommentaar wat deur 'n aanlyn kommentaarplatform gestuur is, aan die platform toegeskryf word eerder as aan die mense wat dit geskryf het. Die rekords van die twee afsonderlike Omgewingsimpakbeoordelingsprosesse moet nie verwar word nie.
  • Die waterstof-/metanol-kompleks, ontsoutingsaanleg, windkragfasiliteit en 132 kV-kraglyn — aansoeke waarmee nog nie begin is nie.
  • Enige aansoek vir die biomassa-aanleg. Die operateur (Ithemba BioEnergy), grondstowwe en skaal is nou gestel; die ligging, waar sowat 200 000 ton biomassa per jaar verkry sou word, en sy eie omgewingsaansoek is steeds nie openbaar nie. Die “groen” aanspraak en die aanleg se koolstofdioksiedvoorsiening hang daarvan af.
  • Die aansoeke om 'n watergebruiklisensie, lisensie vir atmosferiese vrystellings en lisensie vir afvalbestuur.
  • Enige ooreenkomste, aansporings of reëlings vir brandstofaankope tussen die ontwikkelaar en die provinsie, die munisipaliteit of staatsinstansies — verkrygbaar deur middel van 'n formele versoek om rekords.

Facts to establish by original work

Feite om deur oorspronklike werk vas te stel

  • A Hopefield economic baseline — neither social assessment has any town-level data (see the specification).
  • The municipal water balance — whether Saldanha Bay has spare drinking-water capacity, or the plant competes with the town for 8 million litres a day; and whether the Hopefield wastewater works can take the new pipeline's load.
  • Prevailing wind at the site relative to town — for dust and the flare plume.
  • Whether the two announced hydrogen-and-desalination projects on the Berg estuary system are connected in any way, and what a combined assessment of the estuary would show.
  • 'n Hopefield ekonomiese basislyn — geeneen van die sosiale evaluerings het enige dorp-vlak data nie (sien die spesifikasie).
  • Die munisipale waterbalans — of Saldanhabaai oorskiet drinkwaterkapasiteit het, of die aanleg met die dorp om 8 miljoen liter per dag meeding; en of die Hopefield-afvalwaterwerke die nuwe pyplyn se las kan dra.
  • Heersende wind by die terrein relatief tot die dorp — vir stof en die fakkel-pluim.
  • Of die twee aangekondigde waterstof-en-ontsoutingsprojekte op die Bergrivier-mondingstelsel op enige manier verbind is, en wat 'n gekombineerde evaluering van die mondingsgebied sou wys.
